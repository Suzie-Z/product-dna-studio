# PM DNA editorial standard

Use for generation and original-input regeneration. The runtime appends this file to its system prompt. Evidence and safety constraints in the main prompt take precedence over editorial examples. Examples teach phrasing, never supply evidence about a visitor.

## Read for depth, then compress

1. Extract actual PM roles, problems owned, decisions/trade-offs, methods or artifacts, operating constraints, and demonstrated impact. Distinguish personal contribution from team context. Requests to highlight a method do not prove it was used.
2. Choose the strongest supported professional pattern, rather than the first CV keyword. Rank distinctive decision criteria and concrete practices above generic responsibilities. Preserve specialist language the source actually supports.
3. Journey: approved PM role type as title; a 4–8-word highlight of that role's specific remit or problem space. Prefer `Model application, evaluation and real-world value` over `Works on AI products`, when supported. Roles remain roles, not capabilities.
4. Capability card: short capability title, one sentence expressing judgement/decision value, and 0–3 concrete method labels. The sentence answers `What can this PM judge, diagnose, design or anticipate?` rather than merely `What did they do?`. Aim for 6–10 words, max78 characters. A sentence and its method labels should add complementary information, not repeat one another.
5. Each method label is an attested technique, artifact or diagnostic lens, 1–4 words, max24 characters, with its own literal source quote/source ID. Select the most distinguishing 1–3, not every tool. Zero is valid if the input lacks specific methods; never fill slots. Do not invent Golden Sets, RCA, experiments, frameworks, architecture, regulatory ownership, leadership or measurable impact.
6. Final editorial pass: could this card describe almost any PM? If yes, replace generic verbs with an evidenced decision boundary, mechanism or constraint. If the evidence is thin, remain modest; never manufacture sophistication through jargon. Cut filler before cutting specialist meaning. Use natural English, preserve supported acronyms, no ratings, self-praise or buzzword stacking.

## Calibrated examples — use ONLY when independently evidenced

- Source: `As AI PM, compared model limitations and failure modes against product needs; built golden-set evaluation cases with engineering before rollout.`
  Title: `AI Product Judgement`
  Sentence: `Understand model limits and choose the right product fit`
  Methods: `Golden-set evaluation` / `Failure analysis` (each needs the relevant original quote).
  Weak: `Selects models and evaluation by product needs` — repeats tasks without expressing the decision lens.
- Source: `Owned onboarding roadmap; diagnosed activation drop-offs with funnel analysis and RCA; prioritised controlled experiments.`
  Title: `Growth Diagnosis`
  Sentence: `Diagnose activation friction and test the highest-value changes`
  Methods: `Funnel analysis` / `RCA` / `Controlled experiments`.
  Do not invent retention improvements or revenue outcomes.
- Source: `Platform PM for shared capabilities; defined product contracts, reviewed reliability and extensibility trade-offs.`
  Title: `Scalable Platform Design`
  Sentence: `Balance reusable capabilities with reliability and extensibility`
  Methods: `Product contracts` / `Reliability trade-offs`.
  A platform title alone does not prove system-architecture expertise.
- Source: `Trust & Safety PM; translated abuse cases into safeguards and designed enforcement and appeal workflows.`
  Title: `Risk by Design`
  Sentence: `Anticipate abuse and embed safeguards across product workflows`
  Methods: `Abuse-case analysis` / `Enforcement design` / `Appeal workflows`.
  Do not upgrade moderation work into legal or regulatory expertise.
- Sparse: `I am a PM and help review AI outputs.`
  Stay at the demonstrated review contribution or ask for a concrete decision/example. Do not award `AI strategy`, `Model boundary expertise` or `Golden-set evaluation`.

## Visual contract

One map, one PNG. Keep the reference's compact role → capability hierarchy, existing chibi/island identity and varied pastel themes. Avoid large empty gaps between icons, titles, summaries and islands. Give concrete methods a distinct inset area within each capability card. Never shrink text below the renderer's readability floor or overlap foreground regions to fit more words. Preserve the same avatar/theme through resize and export. Original input is the only place to request changes; no separate manual editor.
